# Nightfold Security Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**Mode:** Full audit, daily confidence gate (only findings at 8/10 or higher)  
**Snapshot:** `64034560bc16e6f53ad8609fda10c37f1ec2ea32`  
**Scope:** Tracked project code, current dependency locks, git history, local runtime tests, and the project-local deployment surface. Generated dependencies and globally installed skills were not treated as application code.

## Executive summary

Nightfold is not safe for real-value play in its current form. The poker evaluator itself is well tested, but the security boundaries around it are incomplete. A caller can choose their own hole cards, substitute a fake board, falsely claim `beatShownRank` against threshold zero, or muck another seat. Independently, the newly added cage gives the relayer enough authority to withdraw all cage liquidity to itself without any deposit, chip balance, or game outcome.

Five critical findings need to be fixed before any public deployment or real-money demo. Three high-severity findings affect the EVM settlement trust boundary and refund liveness. Existing happy-path tests pass but do not exercise these adversarial paths.

## Findings

### [CRITICAL] NF-001: Relayer can drain the entire cage without a deposit or game outcome

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A01 Broken Access Control / A08 Integrity Failures  
**Category:** STRIDE Elevation of Privilege / Tampering  
**Location:** `evm/NightfoldCage.sol:107`

**Description:**
`cashOut` trusts the relayer to supply an arbitrary withdrawal ID, recipient, and chip amount. There is no chip ledger, signed withdrawal authorization, Midnight attestation, deposit reference, or cross-chain burn proof. `settledWithdrawals` prevents only reuse of the same caller-chosen ID. A compromised or malicious relayer can use a fresh ID and send the cage's entire native-asset balance to itself. The same relayer can also call `markCredited` without proving that chips were issued, permanently disabling a depositor's reclaim path.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. The attacker obtains the relayer key.
2. They read the cage balance and conversion rate.
3. They choose a fresh `withdrawalId`, their own address, and enough `chips` to consume the balance.
4. `cashOut` passes every check and transfers the whole cage balance.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"funded":"1000000000000000000","depositOrOutcomeCreated":false,
 "recipient":"relayer","remaining":"0"}
```

The proof deployed a fresh cage, funded it with 1 ETH, created no deposit or outcome, and emptied it through the configured relayer.

**Remediation:**
Make withdrawals verifiable rather than discretionary. At minimum, bind each withdrawal to a canonical cross-chain message containing domain/chain ID, cage address, withdrawal ID, player, chip amount, nonce, and source ledger root; require a threshold signature or verifiable proof over that message; and enforce a global chip conservation invariant. Separate credit and withdrawal authorities, add replay protection across all cages rather than per cage, and do not let the relayer choose the beneficiary or amount. `markCredited` must likewise require proof of an actual credit.

**Priority:** P0 (fix now)

### [CRITICAL] NF-002: Any caller can resolve another seat, including forcing an opponent to muck

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A01 Broken Access Control  
**Category:** STRIDE Spoofing / Elevation of Privilege  
**Location:** `contracts/nightfold.compact:76`, `contracts/nightfold.compact:100`, `contracts/nightfold.compact:154`, `contracts/nightfold.compact:167`

**Description:**
The Compact contract does not bind seat 0 or seat 1 to a public key, wallet, or authorization secret. `muckHand` is especially exposed because it consumes no private witness at all: any caller who knows the public `handId` and seat number can mark that seat as mucked. The same missing identity boundary affects deal commitment and showdown actions.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. An attacker observes a public hand ID and waits for one player to reveal.
2. The attacker calls `muckHand(handId, opponentSeat)` with empty private state.
3. The circuit accepts the call because only commitment existence is checked.
4. `settle` treats the victim as having conceded and awards the pot to the other seat.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"unauthorizedMuck":{"usedEmptyPrivateState":true,"winner":"0"}}
```

**Remediation:**
Bind every hand to two seat authorization keys at creation. Require each state transition to prove or disclose a signature/ownership relation for the matching seat and hand ID. Domain-separate signatures by contract address, network, hand ID, seat, action, and nonce. Ensure resolution actions are mutually exclusive and immutable once chosen.

**Priority:** P0 (fix now)

### [CRITICAL] NF-003: Players choose their own hole cards; the dealer commitment is not enforced

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A04 Insecure Design / A08 Integrity Failures  
**Category:** STRIDE Tampering  
**Location:** `contracts/nightfold.compact:76`, `src/game/dealer.mjs:93`

**Description:**
`commitDeal` hashes whatever two private cards the caller supplies. It does not verify that those cards came from the dealer's committed deck, are assigned to that seat, are unique across seats, or are consistent with the published deck commitment. The off-chain `deal()` module creates a deck commitment, but that value never enters the Compact contract. A player can therefore commit pocket aces—or any desired cards—regardless of the actual deal.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. A player receives ordinary cards from the dealer.
2. Instead of committing those cards, they stage stronger cards and a fresh salt.
3. `commitDeal` accepts the self-selected cards because they are locally well formed.
4. At showdown, the player opens the self-created commitment and proves the chosen hand.

**Evidence:**
The end-to-end tests directly stage hard-coded hole cards before calling `commitDeal`; no deck commitment or deal proof is passed to the circuit. The only checks at lines 77–85 are card shape, within-hand uniqueness, and absence of a prior seat commitment.

**Remediation:**
Commit the canonical deck and seat assignments into the hand state. Require an inclusion/opening proof that each hole card occupies the correct committed deck position, or use an authenticated dealer signature over `(domain, handId, seat, card commitments, deck commitment)`. Enforce card uniqueness across both seats and the board through the committed deck permutation.

**Priority:** P0 (fix now)

### [CRITICAL] NF-004: Showdown board is caller-controlled and not bound to the hand

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A04 Insecure Design / A08 Integrity Failures  
**Category:** STRIDE Tampering  
**Location:** `contracts/nightfold.compact:100`, `contracts/nightfold.compact:167`

**Description:**
Both `revealHand` and `beatShownRank` accept the five board cards as call arguments and check only that each card encoding is valid. The contract never stores or verifies the hand's public board or deck commitment. Each player can therefore prove against a different, invented board.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. The honest public board would make the attacker lose to a pair of aces.
2. The attacker supplies a different board completing a royal flush with their committed hole cards.
3. The honest player reveals against the real board.
4. `settle` compares the unrelated ranks and pays the attacker.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"unboundBoard":{"fakeRank":"6722018",
 "honestOpponentRank":"1409269","winner":"0"}}
```

**Remediation:**
Store one canonical board commitment per hand, bind it to the committed deck, and require both showdown circuits to open or reference exactly that board. The board, deck commitment, hand ID, and seat assignments must share one authenticated hand-domain object.

**Priority:** P0 (fix now)

### [CRITICAL] NF-005: `beatShownRank` compares against an attacker-selected threshold

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A04 Insecure Design / A08 Integrity Failures  
**Category:** STRIDE Tampering  
**Location:** `contracts/nightfold.compact:167`

**Description:**
The circuit accepts `threshold` from the caller and never checks that it equals the opponent's recorded `shownRanks` value. It also does not require the opposing seat to have shown. A weak hand can pass threshold zero, record `beatShown`, and be selected as winner by `settle`.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. Seat 0 publicly proves a flush with rank 4,327,921.
2. Losing seat 1 calls `beatShownRank` with threshold `0`.
3. Any valid poker hand evaluates above zero, so the assertion succeeds.
4. `settle` sees `beat1` and awards seat 1 the pot.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"strongRank":"4327921","attackerThreshold":"0",
 "winner":"1","exploit":true}
```

**Remediation:**
Remove `threshold` from the public API. Derive the opponent key from `seat`, require that the opponent has shown, load the threshold from `shownRanks`, and compare against that value. Also prevent `beatShown`, `muckedSeats`, and `shownRanks` from overlapping for the same seat.

**Priority:** P0 (fix now)

### [HIGH] NF-006: EVM escrow accepts arbitrary relayer outcomes and attestations

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A01 Broken Access Control / A08 Integrity Failures  
**Category:** STRIDE Tampering / Elevation of Privilege  
**Location:** `evm/NightfoldEscrow.sol:98`

**Description:**
The escrow verifies only that `msg.sender` is the relayer and `winner <= 2`. It does not verify that `attestation` is nonzero, corresponds to `handId`, commits to `winner`, or exists on Midnight. Recording an arbitrary attestation makes a false settlement detectable after the fact but does not protect funds. A compromised relayer can award every live pot incorrectly; a relayer colluding with a seated account can steal the pot.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. Two players fund a hand.
2. A compromised relayer calls `settle(handId, chosenWinner, arbitraryBytes32)`.
3. The escrow permanently transfers the pot before anyone can challenge it.

**Evidence:**
Lines 102–107 perform only status/range checks before marking the hand settled. The test itself passes a locally invented `keccak256("nf:payout:hand1:seat1")` rather than a verified Midnight proof.

**Remediation:**
Use a verifiable bridge/light-client proof, or at minimum a threshold oracle plus challenge period and bonded slashing. Hash the exact domain-separated settlement payload on Midnight and verify the same payload before payout. Add a pause/rotation mechanism governed by a multisig and cap risk per relayer epoch.

**Priority:** P0 (before real funds)

### [HIGH] NF-007: Privacy-preserving showdown outcomes cannot be relayed

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 6 — Integration Audit / Phase 10 Denial of Service  
**Category:** STRIDE Denial of Service  
**Location:** `src/relayer.mjs:15`

**Description:**
`readOutcome` returns `null` unless both seats published ranks. That makes the advertised `muckHand` and `beatShownRank` paths impossible to relay even after Midnight has marked the hand settled and written `payoutAttest`. Funds remain in the escrow until timeout. This contradicts the main privacy path and turns successful private settlement into a one-hour liveness failure.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. One player shows; the other legitimately mucks or proves a private beat.
2. Midnight settles and writes an attestation.
3. The relayer sees that one `shownRanks` entry is absent and returns `null`.
4. No EVM settlement occurs; users must wait for timeout.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"midnightSettled":true,"relayerOutcome":null}
```

**Remediation:**
Store the winner (or a binding winner commitment that the relayer can read) alongside `payoutAttest`, and have the relayer verify that the attestation commits to `(domain, handId, winner)`. Support all resolution combinations explicitly and add adversarial integration tests for show/muck, show/beat, fold, and split.

**Priority:** P1 (this sprint)

### [HIGH] NF-008: A rejecting seat contract can permanently block escrow refunds

**Confidence:** 10/10  
**Phase:** Phase 9 — OWASP A10 Mishandling Exceptional Conditions  
**Category:** STRIDE Denial of Service  
**Location:** `evm/NightfoldEscrow.sol:124`

**Description:**
`timeout` pushes both refunds in one transaction. If either seat is a contract that rejects native-asset transfers, `_pay` reverts and rolls back both the status change and the other player's refund. The rejecting seat can therefore trap the honest player's stake indefinitely. Split-pot settlement has the same failure mode.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. A malicious contract opens or joins a hand and rejects incoming native asset.
2. The other player matches the stake.
3. After the deadline, the honest player calls `timeout`.
4. Payment to the malicious seat reverts, so neither refund completes and the escrow remains funded forever.

**Evidence:**

```text
{"timeoutReverted":true,"escrowBalance":"20000000000000000",
 "expectedLocked":"20000000000000000"}
```

**Remediation:**
Use pull payments: record `withdrawable[address]` balances, complete the hand state transition without external calls, and let each recipient withdraw independently. Consider allowing withdrawal to a nominated recipient and add reentrancy protection around the withdrawal function.

**Priority:** P1 (this sprint)

### [MEDIUM] NF-009: Private-state encryption falls back to a public password and state paths are not ignored

**Confidence:** 9/10  
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Secrets Archaeology / Phase 11 Data Classification  
**Category:** OWASP A02 Security Misconfiguration / A04 Cryptographic Failures  
**Location:** `src/midnight/providers.mjs:49`, `.gitignore:1`

**Description:**
The private witness store contains hole cards and salts, but the provider silently uses a repository-public fallback password whenever `NIGHTFOLD_STATE_PASSWORD` is absent. The default `.nightfold-state` and `.probe-state` directories are not in `.gitignore`. A non-local invocation that forgets the environment variable gets encryption with a known password, and a resulting state directory can be committed accidentally.

**Exploit scenario:**
1. A player runs the provider without the password environment variable.
2. Their private LevelDB is encrypted with the known fallback.
3. The directory is copied, backed up, or committed.
4. Anyone with the files can use the public fallback to recover private witness data.

**Evidence:**
Line 62 uses `process.env.NIGHTFOLD_STATE_PASSWORD ?? 'Nightfold-Devnet-2026-local'`; the relevant state paths are absent from `.gitignore`.

**Remediation:**
Fail closed when `env.networkId` is not the explicit local devnet. Require a secret from a keychain/KMS or an interactive secret provider, generate per-wallet salts/keys, and ignore `.nightfold-state/` and `.probe-state/`. Document rotation and deletion behavior.

**Priority:** P1 (before multi-user testing)

### [LOW] NF-010: Development infrastructure is not reproducibly pinned and the proof proxy binds broadly

**Confidence:** 9/10  
**Phase:** Phase 3/4/5 — Supply Chain and Infrastructure  
**Category:** OWASP A02 Security Misconfiguration / A03 Supply Chain Failures  
**Location:** `compose.yml:13`, `scripts/proof-proxy.mjs:16`

**Description:**
Docker images use mutable version tags rather than immutable digests. The diagnostic proxy calls `.listen(LISTEN)` without a host, which normally binds all interfaces even though its message says `127.0.0.1`; it has no authentication or request-size limit. On a shared development network this can expose the local proof service and allow memory/CPU denial of service.

**Exploit scenario:**
An attacker on the same network connects to port 6301, sends oversized or expensive proof requests, and reaches the local proof server through the unauthenticated proxy.

**Evidence:**
Compose uses `midnightntwrk/*:<tag>` for all three services. The proxy listener omits the host argument and buffers the entire request in memory before forwarding.

**Remediation:**
Pin container images by digest, bind the proxy explicitly to `127.0.0.1`, enforce request and response size limits/timeouts, and keep the proxy disabled outside diagnostics.

**Priority:** P2 (this month)

Keep `solc` build-only and resolve the advisory without blindly applying the suggested breaking downgrade.

## Remediation roadmap

### P0 — before any real-value deployment (estimated 4–8 engineering days plus protocol review)

1. Redesign cage withdrawals around verifiable, domain-separated messages and a conserved global chip ledger (NF-001).
2. Add per-seat authorization and exclusive lifecycle transitions to the Compact hand state (NF-002).
3. Bind hole cards and board to one authenticated deck commitment (NF-003, NF-004).
4. Derive the private-beat threshold from the opponent's on-ledger rank (NF-005).
5. Replace blind single-relayer EVM settlement or add a threshold/challenge design (NF-006).

These are authorization, conservation, and state-machine invariants. Tests alone leave material uncertainty; use formal specifications/property tests, and consider the community `qedgen-formal-verification` skill for Lean/Kani/proptest artifacts.

### P1 — this sprint (estimated 1–3 engineering days)

1. Relay muck/beat outcomes correctly and cover every resolution matrix entry (NF-007).
2. Convert escrow payouts/refunds to pull payments (NF-008).
3. Fail closed for non-local witness-store passwords and ignore state directories (NF-009).

### P2 — this month (estimated 2–4 hours)

1. Digest-pin Docker images and harden the diagnostic proxy (NF-010).
2. Declare `tsx` in the UI dev dependencies and make the full test command hermetic.
3. Add CI with typecheck, unit/adversarial tests, dependency audit, and artifact checks.
